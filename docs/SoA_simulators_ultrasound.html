Foundational ultrasound simulators (pre-2014)

SCOPUS QUERY
TITLE-ABS-KEY ( ( "ultrasound simulation" OR "simulated ultrasound" OR "B-mode simulation" OR "simulating ultrasound" OR "k-Wave" ) ) AND PUBYEAR < 2014
#Authors, YearTitleSourceFamilyAbstract (summary)DOI
1.1 Bamber & Dickinson1980 Ultrasonic B-scanning: a computer simulation Phys. Med. Biol. 25(3) Convolution The 1980 seminal paper that first numerically simulated a clinical B-mode image. Tissue is modelled as a population of point scatterers and the echo signal is generated through a 1-D point-spread-function convolution. Defines the convolution-based family of simulators that all CT-to-US methods (Shams, Wein) build upon. 10.1088/0031-9155/25/3/004
1.2 Jensen1996 Field: A program for simulating ultrasound systems Med. & Biol. Eng. & Computing 34 (Suppl 1) Wave-based Foundational Matlab simulator that uses spatial impulse responses to model linear ultrasound systems with arbitrary transducer geometries, apodisation and excitation. Supports pulse-echo and continuous-wave fields, and remains the de-facto reference cited by virtually every successive simulator paper in the wave-based and convolution-based families. —
1.3 Jensen2004 Simulation of advanced ultrasound systems using Field II IEEE ISBI 2004 Wave-based A 2004 update of Field II describing how to simulate advanced linear imaging schemes such as synthetic-aperture and coded excitation. Demonstrates parallelisation across a 32-CPU cluster reducing simulation time from 17 months to 391 hours. This is the modern citation key for Field II in 2014+ literature. 10.1109/ISBI.2004.1398618
1.4 Shams, Hartley, Navab2008 Real-Time Simulation of Medical Ultrasound from CT Images MICCAI 2008 LNCS 5242 Convolution Foundational paper splitting CT-to-US simulation into a one-shot pre-processing phase that builds a fixed-view 3D scattering volume from a segmented CT, and a run-time phase that traces the transducer plane through the volume and adds acoustic artefacts. It is the canonical reference for any pipeline that turns a CT volume into B-mode images at interactive frame rates. 10.1007/978-3-540-85990-1_88
1.5 Wein et al.2008 Simulation and fully automatic multimodal registration of medical ultrasound Med. Image Analysis 12(5) Convolution Derives an acoustic impedance proxy from CT Hounsfield Units and convolves it with a depth-dependent point-spread-function to generate B-mode images that register against real intra-operative ultrasound. Together with Shams 2008 it underpins the entire CT-to-US convolution literature; demonstrated for liver and kidney registration at the time of MICCAI 2007 / MIA 2008. 10.1016/j.media.2008.06.006
1.6 Treeby & Cox2010 k-Wave: MATLAB toolbox for the simulation and reconstruction of photoacoustic wave fields J. Biomedical Optics 15(2) Wave-based Open-source Matlab toolbox for the time-domain simulation of full nonlinear acoustic wave propagation in heterogeneous media. Uses k-space pseudospectral methods on regular grids and supports both CPU and GPU back-ends. Adopted alongside Field II as the standard wave-based reference for both ultrasound and photoacoustic simulation. 10.1117/1.3360308
1.7 Bürger et al.2013 Real-Time GPU-Based Ultrasound Simulation Using Deformable Mesh Models IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 32(3) Ray-based GPU ray-tracing simulator over deformable triangle meshes (>2M triangles, up to 200k deformed per frame) with NVIDIA PhysX-driven soft-tissue deformation. Models attenuation, refraction, range distortion and acoustic shadowing in real time. The architecture that NVIDIA Isaac for Healthcare implicitly modernises in 2025 with OptiX raytracing. 10.1109/TMI.2012.2234474

2.1 Mattausch O.2017 Comparison of texture synthesis methods for content generation in ultrasound simulation for training Progress in Biomedical Optics and Imaging - Proceedings Ray-based / texture Compares three procedural texture-synthesis strategies that fill in micro-structure (speckle, fibrosis, fat) inside a ray-traced simulator. Useful as a reference for the texture-on-organ step that any ray-based pipeline (including Isaac) needs. 10.1117/12.2250604
2.2 Mattausch O.2018 Realistic ultrasound simulation of complex surface models using interactive Monte-Carlo path tracing Computer Graphics Forum Ray-based Mattausch and Goksel introduce interactive Monte-Carlo path tracing on triangle-soup surface meshes. Adds multiple-scattering, reverberation and refraction artefacts that Bürger 2013 could not capture, while still running interactively on commodity GPUs. Effectively the immediate predecessor of the 2025 Isaac/UltraRay generation. 10.1111/cgf.13260
2.3 Satheesh B.A.2018 A method of ultrasound simulation from patient-specific CT image data: A preliminary simulation study Proceedings - International Symposium on Biomedical Ima Scatterer-based / CT Preliminary CT-to-US scatterer simulation. Each CT voxel is converted to a population of scatterers whose density and amplitude depend on the local Hounsfield value, then Field-II-like beamforming generates the B-mode image. A direct ancestor of the workflow envisaged for this thesis. 10.1109/ISBI.2018.8363853
2.4 Starkov R.2019 Ultrasound simulation with deformable and patient-specific scatterer maps International Journal of Computer Assisted Radiology an Scatterer-based Starkov, Tanner and Goksel build patient-specific scatterer maps from MR/CT volumes and deform them with FEM. The ultrasound image is then generated by interrogating these deformable scatterer maps, bridging the gap between purely surface-based ray simulators and volumetric scatterer simulators à la Field II. 10.1007/s11548-019-02054-5
2.5 Satheesh B. A.2019 A fast method for simulating ultrasound image from patient-specific CT data Biomedical Signal Processing and Control Scatterer-based / CT Extension of Satheesh & Thittai 2018 with a GPU-accelerated convolution kernel reducing per-frame simulation from minutes to seconds. Validated on liver phantoms and on Visible Human CT data; the closest published method to what we want to implement on the breast biopsy phantom. 10.1016/j.bspc.2018.10.003

Learning-based simulators (GAN, diffusion, NeRF)

SCOPUS QUERY
TITLE-ABS-KEY ( "ultrasound simulation" OR "ultrasound image synthesis" OR "synthetic ultrasound" OR "B-mode simulation" ) AND PUBYEAR > 2014

3.1 Hu Y.2017 Freehand ultrasound image simulation with spatially-conditioned generative adversarial networks Lecture Notes in Computer Science (including subseries Learning / GAN Hu et al. (UCL) condition a GAN on the 6-DoF probe pose to generate spatially consistent freehand sweeps from anatomical models. One of the first works to address the temporal coherence problem of GAN-based US simulation, which is essential for any robotic application that streams images. 10.1007/978-3-319-67564-0_11
3.2 Tom F.2018 Simulating patho-realistic ultrasound images using deep generative networks with adversarial learning Proceedings - International Symposium on Biomedical Ima Learning / GAN seminal Tom and Sheet present the first GAN-based US simulator able to inject realistic pathology (cysts, lesions) into healthy ultrasound images. Trained on intravascular and abdominal data; introduces the patho-realistic GAN concept that subsequent works (spGAN, ConPres) extend. 10.1109/ISBI.2018.8363780
3.3 Peng B.2019 A Real-Time Medical Ultrasound Simulator Based on a Generative Adversarial Network Model Proceedings - International Conference on Image Process Learning / GAN real-time Peng et al. demonstrate the first real-time (~15 fps) GAN-based US simulator running on a single GPU. Targets training of sonographers but the architecture is reusable for robotic applications that need a fast image synthesiser in the loop. 10.1109/ICIP.2019.8803570
3.4 Bargsten L.2020 SpeckleGAN: a generative adversarial network with an adaptive speckle layer to augment limited training data for ultrasound image processing International Journal of Computer Assisted Radiology an Learning / GAN speckle Bargsten and Schlaefer add an explicit physics-inspired speckle layer to a GAN, so the network learns appearance and speckle separately. The augmented images significantly improve downstream segmentation accuracy when training data is scarce. 10.1007/s11548-020-02203-1
3.5 Gilbert A.2021 Generating Synthetic Labeled Data from Existing Anatomical Models: An Example with Echocardiography Segmentation IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging Learning / GAN Gilbert et al. (KCL) generate paired image+segmentation training data by simulating echocardiograms from anatomical models then translating them with a CycleGAN. Closes the loop between physics-based and learning-based simulation, a template the thesis can directly reuse. 10.1109/TMI.2021.3051806
3.6 Liang J.2022 Sketch guided and progressive growing GAN for realistic and editable ultrasound image synthesis Medical Image Analysis Learning / GAN sketch-guided Liang et al. (spGAN) is the most-cited modern US-synthesis paper (123 citations). The user provides an editable anatomical sketch and the GAN generates a realistic US image that follows it, supporting iterative shape editing of lesions and organs - very relevant for thesis-driven dataset augmentation. 10.1016/j.media.2022.102461
3.7 Stojanovski D.2023 Echo from Noise: Synthetic Ultrasound Image Generation Using Diffusion Models for Real Image Segmentation Lecture Notes in Computer Science (including subseries Learning / Diffusion Stojanovski et al. (KCL) introduce the first diffusion-model-based US generator, conditioned on segmentation masks. Outperforms GAN baselines for downstream segmentation accuracy and inaugurates the diffusion family in US synthesis (2023+). 10.1007/978-3-031-44521-7_4
3.8 Wysocki M.2023 Ultra-NeRF: Neural Radiance Fields for Ultrasound Imaging Proceedings of Machine Learning Research Learning / NeRF Wysocki, Navab et al. (TUM) adapt Neural Radiance Fields to ultrasound by replacing the optical rendering equation with an acoustic one. Given a sparse set of real US sweeps with poses, the network learns a continuous volumetric representation that synthesises new views, enabling sim-to-real transfer. —
3.9 Freiche B.2025 Ultrasound Image Generation using Latent Diffusion Models Progress in Biomedical Optics and Imaging - Proceedings Learning / Diffusion Latent diffusion model trained on US data for unconditional and conditional generation. Demonstrates higher fidelity than pixel-space diffusion at lower compute cost; one of the most recent works (2025). 10.1117/12.3047788
